03 /

Why AnythingLLM leads Recall and MRR

Separate what the artifacts prove from what the architecture merely suggests.

Direct evidence

Broader per-query coverage

AnythingLLM beat RAGZ single on Recall for 10 of 12 answerable queries and RAGZ multi on 8 of 12. It beat RAGZ single on nDCG for 10 queries and multi on 11.

Direct evidence

Relevant evidence appears earlier

AnythingLLM placed a relevant interval at rank one on nine queries. Against RAGZ multi it won MRR on q01, q02, and q11, tied nine, and lost none.

Strong inference

Embedding quality matters

AnythingLLM used `text-embedding-3-small`; RAGZ used a deterministic hashed unigram vector plus BM25. Semantic paraphrases and comparisons are naturally easier for the hosted embedding model.

Plausible contributor

Chunking and index geometry differ

AnythingLLM's native chunker and LanceDB ranking can distribute candidates differently from RAGZ heading chunks and dense+sparse RRF. The current experiment changes these together, so their individual contribution is unknown.

Not the cause

Candidate-depth artifact was corrected

The final run retrieved 50 AnythingLLM chunks and 20 RAGZ chunks, then deduplicated to five intervals. Every observation filled all five intervals, so the lead is not caused by collapsing a five-chunk list into fewer intervals.

Metric caution

Twenty pages is forgiving

A hit means the correct twenty-page interval was found, not the exact evidence page. AnythingLLM cannot be credited with page-accurate citation quality from this table.

Where the lead is largest

AnythingLLM's strongest Recall advantages appear on DNS (q06: 1.00 vs 0.25 single / 0.75 multi), hidden terminals (q05: 0.75 vs 0.25 / 0.50), distance-vector failure (q03: 0.80 vs 0.60 / 0.40), and NAT (q12: 1.00 vs 0.667 / 0.667). RAGZ multi leads AnythingLLM on Go-Back-N versus Selective Repeat (q02) and HTTP multiplexing (q11).

Audit of the other Codex report

The report is well designed, links are intact, and it discloses most boundaries. These are the claims that need context.

Supported
“RAGZ indexes 4,412 / 4,412 physical pages.”
Supported by the post-fix ingestion campaign and the restored deep-page retrieval evidence.
Config-specific
“AnythingLLM is 6.20× faster at ingestion.”
Supported for tuned AnythingLLM with hosted OpenAI embeddings versus the complete RAGZ hosted-embedding pipeline. It is not a general native-MiniLM or universal product speed claim.
Coarse metric
Both products have document Recall/nDCG of 1.0.
With only three source documents, document-level retrieval is easy and cannot distinguish page-level evidence quality. AnythingLLM's exact-page result is unavailable.
Adapter result
AnythingLLM answer pass is 87.5%.
That completed path is native AnythingLLM index → external Cohere → external OpenAI answer. Native AnythingLLM chat OOM-killed and has no completed score.
Supported
Onyx is resource-gated.
Correct. The Docker VM exposes roughly 3.6 GiB versus Onyx Standard's documented 10 GB minimum. A numeric zero would be dishonest.
Dataset-specific
“Cohere was latency, not a quality win.”
Correct for that complete large-PDF index: exact-page hit stayed at 55% while median latency rose about 738 ms. It should not be generalized to every corpus or candidate set.

Most misleading line if read alone

The hero says all three products were “tested.” Operationally, RAGZ was fully run, AnythingLLM had a mixture of completed tuned/external and failed native paths, and Onyx received only a resource preflight. The body explains this, but the hero compresses those statuses too aggressively.

The experiment that would identify the cause

The current leaderboard is useful for product selection, but not causal attribution. Run a controlled ablation next.

Equalize evidence units and corpus.
Keep the same 131 intervals and 15 query IDs.
Run RAGZ with `text-embedding-3-small`.
This isolates how much of the gap comes from hash dense vectors versus semantic embeddings.
Keep query mode single first.
Compare AnythingLLM and RAGZ without adding expansion as a second variable.
Then toggle only RAGZ multi-query.
Use live and fixed variants as separate rows, recording expansion tokens and latency.
Calibrate abstention separately.
AnythingLLM's empty-result threshold 0.0 and RAGZ's cosine 0.42 are not comparable policies.
Run Onyx only on a compliant host.
At least 10 GB Docker RAM; keep basic search and agent expansion as separate rows.
