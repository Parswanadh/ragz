Do not merge the campaigns

The other Codex report and the networking benchmark are complementary, not interchangeable.

Campaign A · other Codex

Large-PDF reliability

4,412 pages

Three non-networking PDFs, 98.1 MB, 24 questions. Primary question: can the product ingest beyond page 1,000, recover deep evidence, cite physical pages, and complete native answers?

RAGZ indexed 100% of pages after the batching fix. AnythingLLM was faster in a tuned hosted-embedding configuration but lacked dependable physical-page attribution. Onyx was resource-gated.

Campaign B · this branch

Networking retrieval ranking

2,582 pages

Three networking textbooks, 131 common twenty-page evidence intervals, 15 queries, and counterbalanced RAGZ order runs. Primary question: how do the systems rank relevant evidence at five unique intervals?

AnythingLLM and RAGZ both used OpenAI text-embedding-3-small at 1,536 dimensions. RAGZ retained BM25 + Qdrant RRF and two fixed alternatives. Onyx and RAGFlow were resource-gated.

04 /

What embedding parity changed

The controlled rerun turns the earlier embedding explanation from inference into evidence.

Direct evidence

The Recall gap nearly disappears

RAGZ multi reaches 0.7244 versus AnythingLLM 0.7272—an absolute gap of only 0.0028 after both use the same OpenAI embedding model.

Direct evidence

RAGZ single wins MRR

RAGZ single records 0.8778 MRR versus AnythingLLM 0.8611. AnythingLLM no longer leads every ranking metric.

Confirmed cause

The hash baseline understated RAGZ

Replacing deterministic hash vectors raised RAGZ single Recall from 0.4970 to 0.6800 and multi Recall from 0.6045 to 0.7244.

Remaining confounders

Chunking and index geometry differ

AnythingLLM keeps its native chunker and LanceDB; RAGZ keeps heading chunks, BM25, Qdrant, and RRF. Those jointly explain the remaining difference but are not isolated here.

Not the cause

Candidate-depth artifact was corrected

The final run retrieved 50 AnythingLLM chunks and 20 RAGZ chunks, then deduplicated to five intervals. Every observation filled all five intervals, so the lead is not caused by collapsing a five-chunk list into fewer intervals.

Metric caution

Twenty pages is forgiving

A hit means the correct twenty-page interval was found, not the exact evidence page. AnythingLLM cannot be credited with page-accurate citation quality from this table.

Corrected conclusion

AnythingLLM still leads aggregate Recall and nDCG, but only narrowly over RAGZ multi. RAGZ single places the first relevant interval slightly earlier on average. Multi-query improves RAGZ coverage while sometimes promoting competing candidates ahead of the first relevant result.

05 /

Audit of the other Codex report

The report is well designed, links are intact, and it discloses most boundaries. These are the claims that need context.

Supported
“RAGZ indexes 4,412 / 4,412 physical pages.”
Supported by the post-fix ingestion campaign and the restored deep-page retrieval evidence.
Config-specific
“AnythingLLM is 6.20× faster at ingestion.”
Supported for tuned AnythingLLM with hosted OpenAI embeddings versus the complete RAGZ hosted-embedding pipeline. It is not a general native-MiniLM or universal product speed claim.
Coarse metric
Both products have document Recall/nDCG of 1.0.
With only three source documents, document-level retrieval is easy and cannot distinguish page-level evidence quality. AnythingLLM's exact-page result is unavailable.
Adapter result
AnythingLLM answer pass is 87.5%.
That completed path is native AnythingLLM index → external Cohere → external OpenAI answer. Native AnythingLLM chat OOM-killed and has no completed score.
Supported
Onyx is resource-gated.
Correct. The Docker VM exposes roughly 3.6 GiB versus Onyx Standard's documented 10 GB minimum. A numeric zero would be dishonest.
Dataset-specific
“Cohere was latency, not a quality win.”
Correct for that complete large-PDF index: exact-page hit stayed at 55% while median latency rose about 738 ms. It should not be generalized to every corpus or candidate set.

Most misleading line if read alone

The hero says all three products were “tested.” Operationally, RAGZ was fully run, AnythingLLM had a mixture of completed tuned/external and failed native paths, and Onyx received only a resource preflight. The body explains this, but the hero compresses those statuses too aggressively.

The next controlled experiments

Embedding identity is now controlled. The remaining experiments should isolate chunking, generated expansion, and product-native orchestration.

Equalize chunk inputs.
Index the exact same 131 interval documents in RAGZ and AnythingLLM to isolate retrieval-engine effects.
Compare fixed and live expansion.
Keep original plus two alternatives in separate deterministic and gpt-5.4-mini-generated rows.
Pin one answer model and prompt.
Use gpt-5.4-mini for shared answer generation; keep native product chat as separate descriptive tracks.
Run RAGFlow on compliant hardware.
Its v0.27.0 stack requires at least 16 GB effective Docker/host RAM plus verified daemon disk and kernel-map settings; current Docker memory is 3.86 GB.
Calibrate abstention separately.
The parity rows intentionally use a zero threshold; a held-out no-answer set is still required.
Run Onyx only on a compliant host.
At least 10 GB Docker RAM; keep basic search and agent expansion as separate rows.
